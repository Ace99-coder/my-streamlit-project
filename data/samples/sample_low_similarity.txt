Urban traffic congestion remains one of the most persistent challenges facing rapidly growing cities across the world. Poorly synchronized traffic signals, inadequate public transportation coverage, and a steady rise in private vehicle ownership all contribute to longer commute times and increased fuel consumption. City planners have experimented with congestion pricing, dedicated bus lanes, and smart traffic-signal systems that adjust in real time based on sensor data, each with varying degrees of success depending on local infrastructure and driver behavior. Community engagement is often cited as a decisive factor in whether such measures are accepted or resisted by residents, since commuters are more likely to support changes they were consulted about during the planning process.
