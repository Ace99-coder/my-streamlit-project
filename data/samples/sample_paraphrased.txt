Artificial intelligence includes a branch called machine learning, which allows computers to learn directly from data instead of following hard-coded rules. Enormous collections of data frequently contain patterns that would be nearly impossible for a person to spot without automated assistance. Neural networks form the foundation of deep learning, a more specialized area of machine learning that draws inspiration from how the human brain is structured. Before any model can be trained, the information it learns from must first be cleaned and organized, because real-world data is rarely free of noise, gaps, or inconsistencies. When a dataset is labeled, meaning every example already has a known correct answer attached, the training approach used is called supervised learning. Algorithms that instead work with unlabeled information and try to uncover structure on their own fall under the category of unsupervised learning. A model that has effectively memorized its training examples rather than learning general patterns will typically struggle when it encounters new, unseen data, a problem commonly known as overfitting.
